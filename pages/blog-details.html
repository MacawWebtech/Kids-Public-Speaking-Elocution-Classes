5 games that turn stage fear into stage cheer

Simple, silly games that help nervous kids enjoy being looked at.

By Sana Qureshi
Stage Presence coach · 28 Sep 2026

Stage fear is normal, even for adults. For kids, it usually isn't about the words; it's about being looked at. These five games make being watched feel fun.

1. The spotlight torch

Dim the lights and give your child a torch. Whoever the torch lands on says one sentence: their favourite food, a silly fact, anything. It turns attention into a game, and the “stage” lasts only five seconds.

2. Statue and speak

Your child freezes like a statue, then “comes alive” to say a line. Freezing builds stillness, which is the foundation of calm body language.

“Confidence is not the absence of nerves. It's knowing you can speak even when your knees wobble.”

3. News reporter

Hand over a hairbrush microphone and ask them to report on dinner as breaking news. Kids love the role, and roles are a safe place to practise a big voice.

A quick tip for parents

Laugh with them, not at them, and resist correcting during the game. Feedback can wait until tomorrow.

4. Two truths and a fib

Thinking on your feet, eye contact and a reason to watch the audience's faces: this classic game trains all three.

5. Applause practice

Sounds silly, but practise receiving applause: bow, smile, say thank you. Many children rush off stage because they don't know what to do when people clap.
